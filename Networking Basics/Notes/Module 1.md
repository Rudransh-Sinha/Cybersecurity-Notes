# Types of Networks

## 1. Small Home (SH) Network

A **Small Home (SH)** network connects a few devices, such as computers, smartphones, printers, and smart TVs, allowing them to communicate and share resources.

### Example

- Home Wi-Fi network
- Laptop connected to a printer

---

## 2. Small Office/Home Office (SOHO)

A **Small Office/Home Office (SOHO)** network enables employees to connect to shared resources and communicate securely from a small office or home.

### Features

- Internet access
- File sharing
- Shared printers
- Remote access

---

## 3. Medium-to-Large (M2L) Network

A **Medium-to-Large (M2L)** network connects hundreds or even thousands of devices across multiple locations.

### Examples

- Universities
- Hospitals
- Corporate offices

---

## 4. World Wide Network (WWN)

A **World Wide Network (WWN)** connects millions of computers and networks across the globe.

The Internet is the largest example of a World Wide Network.

---

# Types of Data

## 1. Volunteered Data

Information intentionally shared by a user.

### Examples

- Name
- Email Address
- Phone Number
- Social Media Profile

---

## 2. Observed Data

Information collected by monitoring a user's activities.

### Examples

- Browsing History
- Website Visits
- Device Location
- Purchase History

---

## 3. Inferred Data

Information generated by analyzing observed and volunteered data.

### Examples

- Shopping Preferences
- Recommended Products
- Interests
- Personalized Advertisements

---

# Bandwidth

Bandwidth is the **maximum capacity** of a communication medium to transmit data over a network.

It is usually measured in:

- bps
- Kbps
- Mbps
- Gbps

### Example

A 100 Mbps internet connection has a maximum bandwidth of 100 Mbps.

---

# Throughput

Throughput is the **actual amount of data** successfully transferred over a network within a given period.

Unlike bandwidth, throughput is affected by:

- Network congestion
- Signal interference
- Hardware limitations
- Latency

---

## Bandwidth vs Throughput

| Bandwidth         | Throughput              |
| ----------------- | ----------------------- |
| Maximum capacity  | Actual data transferred |
| Theoretical value | Practical value         |
| Usually higher    | Usually lower           |

---

## Note

In most home internet connections:

- Download speed is higher than upload speed.
- Actual throughput is generally lower than the available bandwidth.

---

# Key Takeaways

- SH networks are designed for homes.
- SOHO networks support small businesses.
- M2L networks connect large organizations.
- WWN connects networks globally.
- Bandwidth represents capacity.
- Throughput represents actual performance.

---

# Interview Questions

### What is Bandwidth?

Bandwidth is the maximum amount of data that can be transmitted over a network in a given time.

---

### What is Throughput?

Throughput is the actual amount of data successfully transferred over a network.

---

### What is the difference between Bandwidth and Throughput?

Bandwidth is the network's capacity, whereas throughput is the actual speed achieved during data transmission.
